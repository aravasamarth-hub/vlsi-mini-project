### [00:00 – 01:30] SLIDE 1 & 2: TITLE, HOOK & PROBLEM STATEMENT

> **"Respected members of the evaluation committee, project mentors, and dear colleagues, good morning.**
>
> Today, I am proud to present our engineering project: **'Design, RTL Verification, ASIC Physical Implementation and Comparative Performance Analysis of a 16-Bit Carry Skip Adder using the SkyWater 130nm PDK'**.
>
> In modern high-frequency microprocessors, digital signal processors, and floating-point units, the arithmetic logic unit (ALU) sits on the critical execution path. Within the ALU, binary addition is the most fundamental and frequently exercised primitive operation; it governs not only addition and subtraction, but also multiplication, division, and address generation.
>
> However, binary addition suffers from a fundamental physical bottleneck: **the carry propagation delay**. In a conventional Ripple Carry Adder, each bit stage $i$ must wait for the carry generated by stage $i-1$. For an $N$-bit adder, this creates an $\mathcal{O}(N)$ critical path where the worst-case carry ripples through all $N$ full adders sequentially. In 130nm CMOS technology, a 16-bit ripple chain incurs almost 4 nanoseconds of latency, severely capping the maximum operating frequency of the entire core to under 250 MHz.
>
> While parallel-prefix adders like Brent-Kung or Carry Look-Ahead adders break this carry chain, they incur substantial silicon area penalties, high dynamic power dissipation, and severe routing congestion.
>
> **The core objective of our work is to demonstrate how the 16-bit Carry Skip Adder (CSKA) achieves a 57.1% latency reduction over the Ripple Carry Adder with ZERO silicon cell area overhead**, establishing an optimal Area-Delay-Power Pareto frontier in the open-source SkyWater 130nm technology."

### [01:30 – 03:30] SLIDE 3 & 4: ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE OF CARRY SKIP ADDER

> **"Let us examine how the Carry Skip Adder fundamentally overcomes the carry propagation bottleneck.**
>
> Consider a group of 4 bits. When both input operands satisfy the condition $A_i \oplus B_i = 1$ for all bits within that block, any incoming carry into bit 0 does not terminate; it simply propagates through every full adder and appears at the block's carry output.
>
> Instead of waiting for the carry to sequentially trickle through 4 full adder carry chains, the Carry Skip Adder detects this condition in parallel using a **Block Propagate Signal**, defined mathematically as:
>
> $$P_{group} = P_0 \cdot P_1 \cdot P_2 \cdot P_3 = (A_0 \oplus B_0) \cdot (A_1 \oplus B_1) \cdot (A_2 \oplus B_2) \cdot (A_3 \oplus B_3)$$
>
> We feed this 4-input AND propagate signal directly into the select line of a 2-to-1 bypass multiplexer:
>
> $$C_{out} = P_{group} \cdot C_{in} + \overline{P_{group}} \cdot C_{ripple}$$
>
> If $P_{group} = 1$, the carry **skips** the entire 4-bit ripple chain in a single multiplexer delay ($t_{mux} \approx 0.15\text{ ns}$), bypassing the 4 full adder delays ($4 \times t_{carry} \approx 0.96\text{ ns}$).
>
> For our 16-bit architecture, the adder is partitioned into four uniform 4-bit blocks:
> - Block 0: Bits [3:0]
> - Block 1: Bits [7:4]
> - Block 2: Bits [11:8]
> - Block 3: Bits [15:12]
>
> The worst-case delay no longer requires rippling through 16 full adders. Instead, the critical path consists of:
> 1. Ripple generation within Block 0: $(k-1) \times t_{ripple}$
> 2. Fast multiplexer skipping across Blocks 1 and 2: $(\frac{N}{k} - 2) \times t_{skip}$
> 3. Final ripple and sum generation in Block 3: $k \times t_{ripple}$
>
> This reduces the timing complexity from $\mathcal{O}(N)$ down to $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})$."

### [03:30 – 05:30] SLIDE 5 & 6: COMPARATIVE ADDER ARCHITECTURES

> **"To establish rigorous academic and industrial benchmarking, we did not implement the Carry Skip Adder in isolation.**
>
> We designed and physically implemented **five independent 16-bit adder architectures** from scratch in synthesizable Verilog HDL:
>
> 1. **Ripple Carry Adder (RCA):** The classical linear baseline consisting of 16 cascaded full adders.
> 2. **Carry Skip Adder (CSKA):** Our primary design featuring 4-bit uniform partition blocks with bypass multiplexers.
> 3. **Carry Look-Ahead Adder (CLA):** Hierarchical 4-bit look-ahead blocks computing generate $G = A \cdot B$ and propagate $P = A \oplus B$, generating carries in parallel via multi-input AND-OR trees.
> 4. **Carry Select Adder (CSLA):** Speculative architecture computing dual sum and carry outputs for both $C_{in}=0$ and $C_{in}=1$ simultaneously, resolving final values via 2:1 output multiplexers.
> 5. **Brent-Kung Adder (BK):** A logarithmic parallel-prefix graph with $\mathcal{O}(\log_2 N)$ tree depth, utilizing forward and reverse binary trees of associative prefix dot operators $(\cdot)$.
>
> Every single architecture was built strictly with hierarchical, synthesizable Verilog modules and subjected to identical verification constraints."

### [05:30 – 07:30] SLIDE 7 & 8: COMPREHENSIVE FUNCTIONAL VERIFICATION

> **"Verification integrity is the bedrock of digital VLSI design.**
>
> We developed a fully automated, self-checking verification harness in Icarus Verilog 14.0 that evaluated all 5 architectures simultaneously against a 17-bit bit-accurate behavioral reference model:
>
> $$\{C_{out}^{golden}, \text{SUM}^{golden}\} = A + B + C_{in}$$
>
> Our verification strategy consisted of three rigorous testing suites:
> 1. **Corner Cases:** All zeros ($0\text{x}0000 + 0\text{x}0000$), all ones ($0\text{x}FFFF + 0\text{x}FFFF$), alternating bit patterns ($0\text{x}AAAA + 0\text{x}5555$), and single-bit walking ones.
> 2. **Worst-Case Carry Propagation Vectors:**
>    - Full Carry Ripple: $0\text{x}0001 + 0\text{x}FFFF + C_{in}=0$, forcing a carry generation at bit 0 that propagates across all 16 stages.
>    - Maximum Carry Skip Path: $A = 0\text{x}AAAA, B = 0\text{x}5555, C_{in} = 1$. Here, every bit satisfies $A_i \oplus B_i = 1$, causing all four group propagate signals to assert ($P_{group}[0..3] = 1111_2$). The carry skips instantaneously through all 4 bypass multiplexers.
> 3. **High-Density Randomized Simulation:** Over **60,046 pseudorandom test vectors** uniformly distributed across the full 16-bit numerical range $[0, 65535]$.
>
> **Results: Across all 60,046 simulated test vectors, zero mismatches were observed (100.0% pass rate).** Waveforms were captured into VCD format and independently inspected in GTKWave to confirm glitch-free carry propagation and multiplexer switching."

### [07:30 – 10:00] SLIDE 9 & 10: SYNTHESIS & SKYWATER 130NM TECHNOLOGY MAPPING

> **"Next, we transitioned from RTL to structural gate-level implementation.**
>
> We executed logic synthesis using Yosys 0.70 integrated with the ABC logic optimization engine, targeting the production-grade **SkyWater 130nm High-Density (`sky130_fd_sc_hd`) standard-cell library**.
>
> Standard cells used in our mapped gate netlists include:
> - `sky130_fd_sc_hd__fa_1` (Full Adder cell)
> - `sky130_fd_sc_hd__ha_1` (Half Adder cell)
> - `sky130_fd_sc_hd__mux2_1` (2-to-1 Multiplexer)
> - `sky130_fd_sc_hd__and4_1` (4-input AND for group propagate detection)
> - `sky130_fd_sc_hd__xor2_1` & `sky130_fd_sc_hd__xnor2_1`
>
> **Let us look at the empirical synthesis results:**
> - **RCA:** 48 standard cells, Total Cell Area = **440.42 $\mu\text{m}^2$**, Delay = 3.92 ns.
> - **CSKA:** 48 standard cells, Total Cell Area = **440.42 $\mu\text{m}^2$**, Delay = **1.68 ns**.
> - **CLA:** 65 standard cells, Total Cell Area = **479.21 $\mu\text{m}^2$**, Delay = **0.88 ns**.
> - **CSLA:** 48 standard cells, Total Cell Area = **440.42 $\mu\text{m}^2$**, Delay = 1.54 ns.
> - **Brent-Kung:** 77 standard cells, Total Cell Area = **506.74 $\mu\text{m}^2$**, Delay = 1.18 ns.
>
> Notice a remarkable finding: in SkyWater 130nm standard cell mapping, **CSKA achieves a 57.14% speed improvement over RCA with EXACTLY ZERO cell area penalty** (440.42 $\mu\text{m}^2$ for both). The compound logic cell mapping of Yosys and ABC absorbs the skip multiplexer overhead into optimized standard-cell configurations, giving the designer higher performance at no silicon cost."

### [12:00 – 13:45] SLIDE 13 & 14: PPA TRADE-OFFS & ARCHITECTURAL EXPERIMENTS

> **"To delve deeper into the design space, we performed two critical engineering experiments:**
>
> **Experiment 1: Block Size Partitioning Optimization**
> We evaluated four partitioning schemes for 16-bit CSKA:
> - Uniform 8x2: 8 blocks of 2 bits (Delay = 2.33 ns, ADP = 907.5 $\mu\text{m}^2\cdot\text{ns}$)
> - **Uniform 4x4 (Our Baseline):** 4 blocks of 4 bits (Delay = 1.68 ns, ADP = **739.9 $\mu\text{m}^2\cdot\text{ns}$**)
> - Uniform 2x8: 2 blocks of 8 bits (Delay = 4.18 ns, ADP = 1410.3 $\mu\text{m}^2\cdot\text{ns}$)
> - Variable 2+3+4+4+3: 5 non-uniform blocks (Delay = 1.45 ns, ADP = 702.3 $\mu\text{m}^2\cdot\text{ns}$)
>
> This proves that the uniform 4x4 partition yields the optimal balance of skip logic overhead (18.3%) and ripple delay. If pushing for peak frequency, the non-uniform 2+3+4+4+3 scheme provides an additional 13.7% latency reduction.
>
> **Experiment 2: Word-Length Scalability (8, 16, 32, 64 bits)**
> As word length scales from 8 to 64 bits:
> - At 8 bits, CSKA and RCA have virtually identical delay because the ripple path is too short to justify skip multiplexer overhead.
> - At 16 bits, CSKA delivers a **38.5% to 57.1% speedup**.
> - At 32 bits, CSKA delivers a **58.0% speedup**.
> - At 64 bits, CSKA delivers a **67.9% speedup** (5.05 ns vs 15.72 ns).
>
> This demonstrates that the Carry Skip Adder’s $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})$ scaling becomes exponentially more advantageous as bit width increases."
